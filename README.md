# Manufacturing energy exposure depends on the output denominator

An industrial analyst screening energy exposure needs to distinguish less production, a different industry mix and a change in energy used per unit of output. A nominal sales/output denominator can also move when prices move. This study will compare those accounting explanations across six euro-area countries using Eurostat physical energy accounts and national accounts.

**Protocol and source-gate stage; no result yet.** The planned comparison uses nineteen non-overlapping manufacturing groups in Belgium, Germany, Spain, France, Italy and the Netherlands from 2015 through 2023. Eurostat metadata discovery verified the needed classifications, gross-output item and price units; the full panel has not yet been retrieved or analysed.

The analysis is a parameter-free accounting decomposition, using an established logarithmic-mean Divisia method. It does not estimate a causal energy-shock effect or equate an accounting intensity term with engineering efficiency. Gross output is a production-value measure with intermediate transactions, not GDP, energy bills or firm profitability. No predictive model or tuning is needed for this question.
